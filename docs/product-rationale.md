# Product rationale

## The opportunity

Proof starts before the polished sentence. A student may have useful contributions from coursework, part-time work, service, or community responsibilities while struggling to recognize what those experiences demonstrate.

The first step is small: capture one thing you actually did, in rough words. Further questions recover decisions, methods, deliverables, and supporting evidence. The app organizes those facts and offers editable sentence structures. It does not supply achievements on the student's behalf.

The durable asset is the evidence collection. A resume version is one selection from it. A contribution can support another application, a skill discussion, or an interview story without requiring the student to reconstruct the experience each time.

This is a product hypothesis about a useful learning workflow, not evidence of improved hiring outcomes or an unoccupied market. Proof combines student-owned evidence, private reflection, transparent local guidance, focused versions, and a self-contained offline application.

## Design decisions

| Student problem | Product response |
| --- | --- |
| “I have no experience.” | Six familiar sources and questions about real responsibilities |
| “I cannot get started.” | One rough memory first; method, outcome, and evidence can follow |
| “One project had several accomplishments.” | Several contribution cards within an experience |
| “I know what I did but not how to say it.” | Action/method/result prompts and editable sentence assembly |
| “I have no impressive numbers.” | Questions about scope, use, quality, decisions, reliability, and deliverables |
| “This sounds like everyone else.” | Details grounded in the student's choices, role, tools, and constraints |
| “It was a simulation, not a live campaign.” | Explicit project stages preserving what was completed |
| “Which evidence belongs in this application?” | Versions with independent selections, wording, and reviews |
| “Where is the proof behind my skills?” | Skill links to contribution cards and student-chosen practice descriptions |
| “Does my work address this requirement?” | Manual mapping with visible phrase and vocabulary suggestions |
| “I need an example I do not yet have.” | Small project plans; accomplishments recorded after completing work |
| “Could I explain this in an interview?” | Private notes, follow-up questions, preparation fields, and a speaking timer |
| “I made a mistake or another tab changed my work.” | Undo/redo, restore points, conflict choices, and recovery downloads |
| “I want to edit elsewhere.” | Editable Word, plain text, browser print, and portable backups |
| “I do not want to upload my data.” | Self-contained HTML, device-local processing, and no external calls |

## Evidence and claim ownership

The student identifies personal work, a shared team contribution, or support for someone else's work. A result may be a deliverable, an observation, or a process someone used. The app does not require a percentage or infer business impact from a completed assignment.

The measurement helper checks arithmetic from supplied before/after values and records the student's measurement basis privately. Correct arithmetic does not establish that a comparison is meaningful or that the student caused the change.

Claim review is a human action. Adapted wording has a separate review for its version. Changing underlying facts or experience details invalidates affected reviews. A phrase match, a connected skill, or a completed checklist cannot substitute for that judgment.

The foundations checklist describes the presence of basic content and selected claim reviews. It does not infer competence, rank applicants, or predict employment.

## Keep the first step light

A novice should not need to understand the whole data model before writing one bullet. Discovery asks for a memory, saves it, and opens its contribution for further work. Provenance, measurement notes, skills, versions, and interview practice are available when useful.

Examples illustrate structure with fictional facts and never prefill a student's resume. Starter projects save private plans rather than presenting planned work as achievement.

## Classroom use

Start with one real moment from an assignment: a decision, revision, analysis, or handoff. Students record their personal contribution before polishing the sentence. Peer reviewers can ask, “What did you do, how did you do it, and how do you know?” A peer review export contains the selected public resume and review questions, not private reflection.

For simulated advertising assignments, useful evidence includes audience rationale, campaign structure, budget decisions, creative work, and a measurement plan. These deliverables do not establish live conversions, revenue, or return on advertising spend. The simulation label remains part of the experience context.

An assignment tool may later offer a local file using the [proof-evidence handoff contract](handoff.md). Proof previews compatible files before adding contributions. Imported claims start unreviewed and stay outside versions until selected. Publishing the contract does not establish an integration with another application or automate grading.

## Public outputs and private work

Resume projections include selected bullets and public education, contact, and skill information. Evidence, reflections, rehearsal notes, and opportunity descriptions remain excluded. Interview preparation has a separate export with an explicit choice to include private notes. Project backups retain the complete working data.

Source modules assemble into one HTML file with no runtime dependencies. This keeps offline use practical and behavior inspectable. Modules also make validation, exports, privacy boundaries, and teaching content easier to review separately while preserving the single page product.
