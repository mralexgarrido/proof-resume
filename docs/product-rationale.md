# Product rationale

## The opportunity

Proof is designed around evidence discovery before prose generation. Students may have useful contributions in course projects, part-time jobs, service, or community responsibilities but lack a way to recognize and describe them. Guided reflection can help them identify the specific actions, methods, and outcomes they can explain in their own words.

A September 2026 spot check of Rezi and Teal's own product pages found prominent positioning around AI writing, job alignment, resume analysis, and scores. Both also discuss achievements. It would be inaccurate to claim that evidence-based bullets are absent from the market. Proof's proposed differentiation is the combination of a student-centered reflection process, private evidence notes, transparent local coaching, no external calls, open source code, and editable Word output. This is a product hypothesis, not proof of an unoccupied market or improved hiring outcomes.

## Design decisions

| Student problem | Product response |
| --- | --- |
| “I have no experience” | Six experience sources with questions about real responsibilities and contributions |
| “I know what I did but not how to say it” | Action, method, outcome prompts and editable sentence assembly |
| “I do not have impressive numbers” | Prompts for deliverables, scope, use, quality, and reliability without fabricated metrics |
| “This sounds like everyone else” | Details grounded in the student's own role, decisions, and tools |
| “Could I defend this in an interview?” | Private evidence notes and an explicit claim-review check |
| “I want to keep editing in Word” | Real OOXML paragraphs, headings, and bullets |
| “I do not want to upload my data” | Self-contained HTML and device-local processing |

The checklist measures the presence of basic content and user-reviewed claims. It does not infer competence, rank applicants, or predict a hiring outcome. Claims are still the user's responsibility.

## Classroom use

Start by asking students to identify one moment when their contribution changed the quality, clarity, reliability, or usefulness of a piece of work. Let them record the evidence before polishing the sentence. Peer reviewers can then ask, “What did you do, how did you do it, and how do you know?” Students should share only the résumé they choose to export, not the private backup containing reflection notes.
